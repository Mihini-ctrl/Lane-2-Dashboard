ChatGPT said:
In recent years, around 85–88% of New Zealand's electricity has come from renewable sources. The main ones are:

Hydro: roughly 55–60%, mostly from the South Island lakes and the Waikato River.
Geothermal: about 18–20%, mainly in the central North Island.
Wind: around 6–7% and growing.
Solar: a small but fast-growing share.

The remainder comes mostly from gas and coal, much of it at the Huntly Power Station. In dry years, when hydro lakes are low, Huntly burns more coal to cover the shortfall, so the renewable share can drop.

You said:
how do road user charges work for EVs and when did they start
ChatGPT said:
Since 1 April 2024, light electric vehicles in New Zealand have had to pay road user charges (RUC), like diesel vehicles. The rate for light EVs is $76 per 1,000 km, and plug-in hybrids pay $38 per 1,000 km. Owners buy RUC in advance in blocks of kilometres and display a RUC licence. Before April 2024, EVs were exempt from RUC as an incentive to encourage uptake.

Yes, that's right. At $76 per 1,000 km, 12,000 km a year comes to 12 × $76 = $912 in road user charges per year.

Here's some feedback on your introduction:

1. No clear position: You say you'll discuss whether EVs are good, but you don't say what you think. An argumentative essay needs a thesis in the introduction.
2. Too general: "Becoming more popular" and "many advantages and disadvantages" could apply to any topic. A specific fact or hook would make it stronger.
3. Signposting: "In this essay I will talk about" is a weak way to introduce your argument. Tell the reader what you will argue, not what you will talk about.
4. Doesn't match the question: The question asks whether EVs are the right *path*, which invites you to compare them with alternatives like public transport.
